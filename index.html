Odds ratios are on the native scale: per 1 unit for counts, scores and depth in centimetres, and presence vs. absence for toe location and aspirin. Wound number and total wounds are strongly correlated and pull in opposite directions, so neither coefficient means much on its own.

How well it performs

Developed on 1,255 diabetic foot ulcers from 422 patients treated between 2012 and 2025, split 70/30 by patient. On the 351 held-out wounds: AUC 0.684 (95% CI 0.61–0.76), which beats WIfI stage alone by 0.121 (95% CI 0.030–0.207, p=0.004). At the 71% cut-point, sensitivity 0.81 and specificity 0.51. Calibration slope 0.72, Hosmer–Lemeshow p=0.099, Brier 0.144.

An AUC of 0.68 is modest. This model separates healing from non-healing wounds better than staging does, but it is not a substitute for clinical judgement.

The equation

A logistic regression with a Platt recalibration learned from out-of-fold predictions on the training set only:

p = 1 / (1 + exp(-(0.116206 + 0.899692 × L)))

L = 1.604873 − 0.565857·WoundNum + 0.505326·TotalWounds − 0.542333·W + 0.685499·Toe + 0.523756·Depth − 0.315045·UTStage + 0.410967·Aspirin

The 71% cut-point is the Youden-optimal operating point on the held-out set, not a 50/50 line, so a wound between 50% and 71% still classifies as not healed.
