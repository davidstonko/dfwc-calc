Odds ratios are on the native scale: per one grade, and presence vs. absence for toe location and noncompressible tibial vessels. An odds ratio above 1 favours healing. The model is the three WIfI component grades, entered separately because they predict healing better than the composite stage they are combined into, plus the two variables that stability selection added. The foot infection grade carries no independent weight once the others are in the model; it is kept so that the model uses all three grades a clinician has already assigned.

Toe wounds heal better than wounds elsewhere on the foot, and that is the largest single effect here. Noncompressible tibial vessels predict healing where the ankle-brachial index does not, which is why the calcification phenotype is asked for and the index value is not.

How well it performs, and where it does not

Developed on 1,141 diabetic foot ulcers from 387 patients treated between 2012 and 2025, split 755/386 by patient. A further 114 wounds were excluded because they had neither a definitive outcome nor 180 days of observation.

On the 386 held-out wounds: AUC 0.700 (95% CI 0.643–0.754). WIfI stage alone reaches 0.625, so the margin is +0.075 (95% CI 0.026–0.124, p=0.001). At the 63% cut-point, sensitivity 62% and specificity 72%, positive predictive value 83% and negative predictive value 47%. Calibration slope 0.99, Brier score 0.196.

The honest caveat. Entering the three WIfI component grades directly, with no model at all, reaches 0.682. This five-variable model reaches 0.700, and the difference (+0.018, 95% CI −0.015 to 0.052) is not statistically significant. Across 23 approaches tested, including tuned gradient boosting, random forests, support vector machines and a neural network over 77 candidate variables, nothing significantly beat the three component grades. Use this as a convenience, not as evidence that modelling adds much.

Calibration. Calibration slope 0.99, Hosmer–Lemeshow 12.6 on 8 degrees of freedom, p=0.13, and no quintile of predicted risk is more than 8 points from the observed rate. Still, read the number as a rank more than as a precise risk: the model was developed and tested at one center.

Developed and internally validated at one center. Not externally validated.

What "healed" means here

The outcome is healing within 180 days of presentation, and every wound in the analysis was observed for at least that long or reached a definitive outcome before it.

This matters more than it sounds. The registry's healing field carries no time horizon, so a wound recorded as open at last contact counts as a failure whether it had been followed for three years or three weeks. Under that definition the median observation was 96.5 days for open wounds against 931 days for healed ones, and the ischemia grade showed no gradient at all (chi-squared 1.64, p=0.65). Anchoring the outcome at 180 days makes the gradient appear: 70.3% healing at I-0 against 41.1% at I-2, chi-squared 38.36, p<0.0001.

Of the 403 wounds not healed by 180 days, 271 healed later, 48 came to major amputation, 43 patients died before the wound resolved, and 41 were still open. So "not healed" here means not healed yet, not healed ever.

The equation

A logistic regression with a Platt recalibration learned from out-of-fold predictions on the training set only:

L = 1.555616 − 0.553123·W − 0.293273·I + 0.051720·fI + 0.780178·Toe − 0.633521·NC

p = 1 / (1 + exp(-(0.046374 + 0.899547 × L)))

W, I and fI run 0 to 3; Toe and NC are 1 or 0. The 63% cut-point is the Youden-optimal operating point on the held-out set (0.6347), not a 50/50 line, so a wound between 50% and 63% still classifies as not healed by 180 days.
